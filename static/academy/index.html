    Cargo operations · beginner lesson

    Read an air waybill and cargo manifest

    Follow one fictional PAC shipment through both documents. Open each sample as you work through the checks below.

    01 · Air waybill

    What was booked?

    The air waybill (AWB) identifies the shipment, its shipper and consignee, route, pieces, weight, charges and handling instructions. In the sample, look for 901-10000123, 10 cartons, and KCVG → KMIA.

    Gross weight is the freight's actual weight: 1,200 kg. Chargeable weight is used to price this shipment: 1,440 kg. The aircraft load uses gross weight, never the billing figure.

    Open sample PAC air waybill (PDF) ↗

    02 · Cargo manifest

    What is on the flight?

    The manifest lists cargo assigned to a flight. Match its AWB number, route, 10 cartons, and 1,200 kg gross against the waybill. The sample places the freight at M01 for this training exercise.

    Both documents show PER · keep chilled 2–8°C. That instruction needs attention during acceptance and loading; it is not a substitute for the aircraft's actual load limits or operator procedures.

    Open sample PAC cargo manifest (PDF) ↗

    03 · Check yourself

    Before you release the load

    Confirm the AWB number, departure and arrival, piece count, gross weight, and special handling agree. Use gross weight in the load sheet. If either document differs, stop and resolve it with dispatch before continuing.

    For this sample, the billing charge is 1,440 × $2.50 = $3,600. That amount never goes into the aircraft weight calculation.

    These are fictional PAC simulator training documents, not shipping paperwork or a real-world loading approval.
